I noticed something slightly embarrassing: I don't even like most of the videos I spend hours watching.

I don't search for them, choose them or remember them.

One ends, another begins, and eventually I look at the time and discover that part of my evening has disappeared.

Nothing happened.

But an hour passed.

## It doesn't feel like entertainment anymore

When I watch a film, read a chapter or play a game, I know what I am doing.

I decided to begin. There is usually a point where I decide to stop. Even when the thing is mediocre, it still feels like I spent time on *something*.

Short videos feel different.

There is rarely a first video. I open an app for another reason, touch the wrong part of the screen, and suddenly someone is explaining a controversy I had never heard about and will never think about again.

Then there is a joke.

Then a product.

Then a person reacting to another person reacting to a clip from somewhere else.

None of it is terrible enough to make me leave. None of it is good enough to have chosen.

That might be the perfect formula.

## I keep waiting for the good one

Every few videos, something genuinely interesting appears.

A scene from a show I love. A useful idea. A person who is actually funny. A small piece of the world I would not have found on my own.

That one good video makes the previous twenty feel like they were leading somewhere.

They weren't.

The next twenty probably aren't either.

Still, my thumb has learned that another chance is one movement away. There is no loading screen, no decision and no cost large enough to notice.

Just one more.

I don't think I am watching because I am entertained. I am watching because leaving requires a clearer decision than continuing.

Continuing is what happens when I do nothing.

## There is no natural place to stop

A book has pages. An episode has credits. A game eventually returns to a menu.

The short-video feed has none of that.

It does not finish because it is not trying to take me anywhere. There is no destination—only the next item.

That makes stopping feel strangely arbitrary.

Why stop after this video?

The next one is already moving before I have answered.

I have closed these apps hundreds of times without feeling finished. It is more like pulling my hand away from a machine that would happily continue without me.

Sometimes I reopen it a few minutes later.

Apparently my brain believes the feed may have produced a satisfying ending while I was gone.

## The videos leave almost nothing behind

This is the part I dislike most.

I can spend a long time scrolling and struggle to name five things I watched.

The videos were bright enough to hold my attention but not solid enough to become memories. They occupied the hour without really joining my day.

A bad film can become a funny conversation. A boring walk can produce a thought. Even staring at the ceiling gives the mind somewhere to wander.

Scrolling leaves me with the feeling that I was busy, without anything I can point to.

Not rested.

Not entertained.

Not exactly unhappy either.

Just used up.

## I have tried making the machine slightly less attractive

Timers. App limits. Greyscale. Moving icons. Turning off notifications.

They all help for a while.

Then I become a lawyer representing myself against rules that I wrote yesterday.

The timer is unreasonable because today was stressful.

The app is necessary because someone might have sent me something.

Five minutes is harmless.

It is impressive how quickly I can construct a legal defence for watching a man rank different brands of bottled water.

The problem is not that I believe short videos are important. The problem is that they are available during every moment when I don't know what else to do.

Waiting for food.

Lying in bed.

Sitting somewhere for two minutes.

Feeling stuck on a task.

The feed has become the default answer to the smallest amount of discomfort.

## I miss the empty parts

I used to think boredom was the problem.

Now I think boredom was doing something.

It gave thoughts enough time to become interesting. It made a song, a conversation or going outside feel like an event. It allowed an ordinary minute to remain ordinary instead of immediately covering it with noise.

I don't want to turn every empty moment into productivity. That sounds exhausting in a different way.

I would just like some of those moments to stay empty.

To wait without being entertained.

To eat without placing a video beside the plate.

To go to bed and experience the deeply unfashionable activity of trying to sleep.

## I don't have a dramatic solution

I am not deleting the internet and moving to a mountain.

I will probably continue watching short videos. Some of them are good. Some of them make me laugh. Pretending otherwise would make this a much cleaner essay and a much less honest one.

But I am trying to notice the moment before the feed begins.

Did I open this because I wanted to see something, or because my hand arrived here before I did?

If I don't know what I came for, that is probably a good time to leave.

Not because every hour needs to become useful.

I just want to be present when it passes.
